Percent vs. Percentage Points: Why It Matters

The same change can sound small or huge depending on the words.

By FinCraftScope Editorial Team·Updated Nov 13, 2026·6 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

A percentage point is the simple difference between two percentages. A percent change is relative. If a rate rises from 2% to 3%, that’s a 1 percentage point increase — but a 50% increase in the rate itself.

STEP / 01

The difference in one example

Interest rates move from 4% to 5%. In percentage points, that’s +1. In percent terms, the rate is 25% higher than before.

Both statements are true; they just answer different questions.

STEP / 02

Where it shows up

Central bank decisions are usually described in percentage points, or in basis points — one basis point is 0.01 percentage point.

Unemployment, tax rates and poll results are also often compared in points.

STEP / 03

Reading headlines carefully

A headline saying a risk “rose 50%” may describe a move from 2 in 1,000 to 3 in 1,000.

Asking “percent of what?” helps you judge whether a change is large in practical terms.

A USEFUL TIP

When you see a percent change, look for the starting number. Without it, the size of the change is hard to judge.

Frequently asked questions

What is a basis point?

One hundredth of a percentage point. A 0.25 point rate change is 25 basis points.

Which is more accurate?

Neither. They measure different things, and good reporting makes clear which one is used.

BEFORE YOU GO FURTHER

This guide explains a general concept; it isn’t a recommendation to buy, sell or borrow. For decisions about your own money, consider speaking with a licensed financial professional.
