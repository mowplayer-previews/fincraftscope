Why a Stock Index Can Rise While Most Stocks Fall

How weighting shapes what an index really tells you.

By FinCraftScope Editorial Team·Updated Nov 13, 2026·8 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Many major indexes are weighted by company size. When a handful of very large companies rise strongly, the index can go up even if most of the smaller companies in it are falling.

In this guide
  1. 01  How weighting works
  2. 02  Narrow vs. broad rallies
  3. 03  Why it matters for investors
STEP / 01

How weighting works

In a market-cap-weighted index, each company counts in proportion to its total market value.

A company worth hundreds of times more than another has hundreds of times more influence on the index.

ADVERTISEMENT
STEP / 02

Narrow vs. broad rallies

When gains come from a few giants, analysts call it a narrow market. When most stocks rise together, it’s broad.

Market breadth measures, like the share of stocks above their average price, help show which is happening.

STEP / 03

Why it matters for investors

An index fund concentrated in a few big names carries more exposure to those companies than many investors realize.

Equal-weighted indexes give every company the same weight and offer a different view of the market.

A USEFUL TIP

Check a fund’s top ten holdings and what share of the fund they represent.

Frequently asked questions

Are all indexes weighted by size?

No. Some are price-weighted or equal-weighted.

Is concentration bad?

Not necessarily, but it means results depend heavily on a few companies.

BEFORE YOU GO FURTHER

This guide explains a general concept; it isn’t a recommendation to buy, sell or borrow. For decisions about your own money, consider speaking with a licensed financial professional.
