What Is Diversification and Why Does It Work?

Spreading risk so one bad bet can’t sink you.

By FinCraftScope Editorial Team·Updated Nov 13, 2026·9 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Diversification means spreading your money across many investments that don’t all move together. When one falls, others may hold steady or rise, which smooths the ride and reduces the chance that a single failure wipes out your savings.

In this guide
  1. 01  The core idea
  2. 02  Ways to diversify
  3. 03  What it can’t do
STEP / 01

The core idea

If you own one company’s shares, your result depends entirely on that company. If you own hundreds, any single one has a small effect.

Risk specific to one company or industry can be largely diversified away; risk that hits the whole market cannot.

ADVERTISEMENT
STEP / 02

Ways to diversify

Across companies and industries, across countries, and across asset types such as stocks, bonds and cash.

Broad index funds are one of the simplest ways to get wide diversification in a single holding.

STEP / 03

What it can’t do

Diversification doesn’t guarantee profits or prevent losses in a broad market decline.

Owning many funds that hold the same things isn’t real diversification — check what’s inside them.

A USEFUL TIP

Rebalance once or twice a year to bring your mix back to its target. It keeps your risk level where you intended.

Frequently asked questions

How many stocks do I need to be diversified?

There’s no magic number, but a broad fund holding hundreds of companies usually does the job.

Does diversification lower returns?

It may mean you never hold only the single best performer, but it greatly reduces the chance of holding only the worst.

BEFORE YOU GO FURTHER

This guide explains a general concept; it isn’t a recommendation to buy, sell or borrow. For decisions about your own money, consider speaking with a licensed financial professional.
