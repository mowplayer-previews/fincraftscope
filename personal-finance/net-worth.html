How to Calculate Your Net Worth — and Why It Matters

One number that shows your whole financial picture.

By FinCraftScope Editorial Team·Updated Nov 13, 2026·9 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Your net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. Tracking it over time shows whether your overall finances are moving in the right direction, beyond any single month’s budget.

In this guide
  1. 01  The simple formula
  2. 02  Why a negative number is common
  3. 03  How to track it
STEP / 01

The simple formula

Add up your assets: cash, savings, investments, retirement accounts and the realistic value of property or a car.

Subtract your liabilities: mortgage, car loan, student loans, credit card balances and any other debt. The result is your net worth.

ADVERTISEMENT
STEP / 02

Why a negative number is common

Early in a career, or after taking on student loans, many people have a negative net worth. That’s a starting point, not a verdict.

What matters most is the direction: paying down debt and saving both push the number up.

STEP / 03

How to track it

Update it once a quarter or twice a year in a simple spreadsheet. Use conservative values for things like cars, which lose value quickly.

Watching the trend helps you see the long-term effect of everyday choices.

A USEFUL TIP

Don’t count things you’d never sell, like furniture, at what you paid. Value assets at what they’d realistically fetch today.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include my home?

Yes, at a realistic market value, with the mortgage listed as a liability.

Is net worth the same as wealth?

It’s the most common way to measure wealth, though it doesn’t capture future income.

BEFORE YOU GO FURTHER

This guide explains a general concept; it isn’t a recommendation to buy, sell or borrow. For decisions about your own money, consider speaking with a licensed financial professional.
