How Credit Card Interest Is Calculated

Grace periods, daily rates and why balances grow.

By FinCraftScope Editorial Team·Updated Nov 13, 2026·9 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Most credit cards charge interest daily on your balance using a daily rate — the APR divided by 365. If you pay the full statement balance by the due date, you usually avoid interest on purchases thanks to the grace period.

In this guide
  1. 01  The grace period
  2. 02  The daily calculation
  3. 03  Why minimum payments are costly
STEP / 01

The grace period

Pay the full statement balance on time and most cards charge no interest on new purchases.

Carry any balance, and you typically lose the grace period, so interest starts on new purchases too.

ADVERTISEMENT
STEP / 02

The daily calculation

A 24% APR is roughly 0.066% per day. Each day, that rate is applied to your balance.

Many issuers use your average daily balance over the billing cycle to calculate the charge.

STEP / 03

Why minimum payments are costly

Minimum payments often cover little more than the interest, so the balance shrinks slowly.

Paying even a bit more than the minimum shortens payoff time and lowers total interest.

A USEFUL TIP

If you carry a balance, pay as early in the cycle as you can. A lower average daily balance means less interest.

Frequently asked questions

Do cash advances have a grace period?

Usually not. Interest typically starts immediately and the rate may be higher.

What is a balance transfer?

Moving debt to a card with a lower, often temporary, rate. Fees usually apply.

BEFORE YOU GO FURTHER

This guide explains a general concept; it isn’t a recommendation to buy, sell or borrow. For decisions about your own money, consider speaking with a licensed financial professional.
