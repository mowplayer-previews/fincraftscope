How Deposit Insurance Protects Your Savings

What’s covered if a bank fails — and what isn’t.

By FinCraftScope Editorial Team·Updated Nov 13, 2026·8 min read
THE SHORT ANSWER

Deposit insurance is a government-backed guarantee that protects bank deposits up to a set limit if a bank fails. In the United States, the FDIC covers up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. Limits and rules differ by country.

In this guide
  1. 01  Why it exists
  2. 02  What’s usually covered
  3. 03  What usually isn’t
STEP / 01

Why it exists

Deposit insurance reduces the risk of bank runs, where many people try to withdraw money at once out of fear.

Knowing deposits are protected helps keep the banking system stable.

ADVERTISEMENT
STEP / 02

What’s usually covered

Checking and savings accounts, and often certificates of deposit, at insured institutions.

Coverage depends on the account ownership category, so joint and individual accounts may be insured separately.

STEP / 03

What usually isn’t

Investments such as stocks, bonds, mutual funds and crypto assets are generally not covered, even if bought through a bank.

Check whether your institution is insured and what the limit is in your country.

A USEFUL TIP

If your savings exceed the limit at one bank, spreading them across insured banks or ownership categories can keep everything covered.

Frequently asked questions

Does it cover crypto exchanges?

Generally no. Deposit insurance applies to deposits at insured banks.

How fast do people get their money?

Insured deposits are typically made available quickly, often within days.

BEFORE YOU GO FURTHER

This guide explains a general concept; it isn’t a recommendation to buy, sell or borrow. For decisions about your own money, consider speaking with a licensed financial professional.
